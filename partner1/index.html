The History & Introduction of Linux

History

I want to begin by telling a story about Linus Torvalds, a Finnish computer science student, who began developing the Linux kernel in 1991. He was inspired by the existing Unix operating system and wanted to create a free, open-source alternative. The first Linux distributors quickly gained popularity among developers and enthusiasts, leading to the growth of a vibrant community that contributed to its development and expansion. Today, Linux powers 100% of the world's top 500 fastest supercomputers and 90% of all web servers, and countless other applications. But for now, enough of that information you can find in any old Linux book. I want to tell the most important tale, one that involves taking back the freedom and control over your computer.

Introduction

The internet and computers are in a very different place than they were 10, 5, or even 2 years ago. The internet is no longer a free and open space for people to share ideas and information. It has become a place where corporations and governments control what we see, hear, and do online. The same is true for our computers. We are no longer in control of our own devices. We are at the mercy of the software and hardware manufacturers who decide what we can and cannot do with our own machines. Fortunately, people are fighting back by choosing open-source alternatives like Linux. Even as a small team, together Linux is building a prosperous future for open computing. There is a future of choice when it comes to operating-system-level AI implementation, forced telemetry, and background ads slopping up computer resources. This site is dedicated to Linux, a site for an alternative to the mainstream operating systems that shall not be named. A site to inspire users that Linux terminal and command-line should not be feared. This is for the revolution!
